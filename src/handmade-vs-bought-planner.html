Handmade or Bought?

Some gifts want your hands. Some want your credit card. Both are full of love. This planner helps you decide which is which before the deadline turns a fun project into a 2 a.m. glue-gun emergency.

How to use it

  1. One row per person. Write the idea, then circle handmade or bought — honestly, based on the time you actually have.
  2. For handmade rows, estimate the real time and supplies cost. Double your first guess; that's usually the true number.
  3. Fill in a start by date on page 3, then let the boxes on this sheet track started / finished.
  4. It's completely allowed to switch a row from handmade to bought halfway. The point is the person, not the process.

What's inside

  • Page 2 — the planner table: idea, route, time, cost, progress.
  • Page 3 — a friendly decision guide, a supplies list, and start-by dates.

A kind note: a homemade gift made calmly says “I thought of you for weeks.” A homemade gift made frantically says “I didn't sleep.” Same glue gun, very different message — plan for the first one.

Print at 100% (“Actual size”), US Letter, portrait. Prints nicely in black & white. Personal use: print as many copies as you like.

Choosing With Your Energy in Mind

Neither column is “better.” The right one is the one you'll still be glad about on the big day.

Lean handmade when…

  • the person values time and attention more than things;
  • you genuinely enjoy making it — even the fiddly parts;
  • it can be made in batches (jam, bread, ornaments) for many people at once;
  • the calendar says you have room. If it doesn't, that's the answer.

Lean bought when…

  • the season is already full and your energy is a real budget line;
  • someone on your list specifically wants a thing only money can find;
  • a small handmade touch (a tag, ribbon, note) can warm up a store-bought gift — hybrid counts.
